---
title: Make it team-ready
description: Take a fresh instance from empty to safe to hand to your team, with a model, a business description, a colleague, and an emergency stop.
---

A fresh instance is signed in and empty. It cannot answer anything yet, it knows nothing about your
business, and you are the only person in it.

By the end of this it will be ready for other people: it can think, it knows what you do, a
colleague can sign in, and you will know exactly how to stop everything if you ever need to.

About fifteen minutes, all of it in the browser.

<Callout type="info">
  No instance yet? [Install one first](/docs/self-hosting/install).
</Callout>

## Before you start

You need an admin account, which is the account you created during setup.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Give it a model

Nothing works until the assistant can think. This is the one step everything else waits on.

Open **Operate → Business → Secrets** and add an API key for a provider. Then open
**Operate → Business → Models** and assign models to the three chains: **Fast**, **Balanced**, and
**Thorough**. Under **What each effort means**, choose which of them **Auto** should use when
nobody picks.

Now open **Chat** and say hello. If it replies, this step is done.

Full walkthrough: [configure LLM providers](/docs/administration/configure-llm-providers). No API
key? A Claude or ChatGPT plan you already pay for works instead. See
[run on a subscription](/docs/administration/run-on-a-subscription).

</Step>

<Step>

### Tell it what your business does

Agents read your business description before they answer anything. An instance that knows you run a
café gives different answers from one that knows you run a law firm.

The **Companion** (the tulip icon in the corner) asks for this. Answer it there, or open
**Operate → Business → About** and write two or three sentences: what you do, who for, and anything
an agent would be embarrassed not to know.

This takes a minute and improves every answer the instance ever gives.

</Step>

<Step>

### Invite a colleague

Open **Operate → Business → People**, choose to invite someone, and enter their email.

They arrive as a **member**, which is the right default. Members can build things, chat, and work
with records, but they cannot reach secrets, model configuration, or operations.

See [invite your team](/docs/administration/invite-your-team) for what an invite contains and how
long it lasts.

</Step>

<Step>

### Check what they can actually do

Do not assume. Ask.

Open **Operate → Business → People & access** and choose the **Check** tab. Pick the person you
just invited, pick an action, and it tells you yes or no.

This is worth doing once so you trust the boundary. Permission in TulipFarm only ever narrows: an
agent can never do more than the person who set it going, and a member cannot gain admin powers by
asking an agent nicely.

More: [manage access](/docs/administration/manage-access).

</Step>

<Step>

### Find the emergency stop before you need it

Open **Operate → Health → Operations** and look at the **Kill switches** panel. Do not arm one,
just find it, and notice that you could stop **every mutating effect** with one button.

That is the whole point of this step. The moment to learn where the stop is, is not the moment you
need it.

Read [emergency stop](/docs/administration/emergency-stop) so you know what arming one does and
does not do.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What you have now

An instance that can think, knows what your business does, has a second person in it, and has a
stop you know how to reach.

That is what *safe to hand to a team* means here.
