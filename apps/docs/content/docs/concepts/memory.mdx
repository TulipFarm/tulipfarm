---
title: Memory
description: Durable, scoped facts an assistant recalls across chats — and what belongs elsewhere.
---

Memory is what an assistant durably recalls about you across chats. It is for preferences and
recurring user-specific facts, not for static business identity or durable business Records.

Memory is made of **Assertions** — one scoped, versioned statement each, carrying where it came
from and how far it is trusted. An Assertion belongs to a **Memory Scope** that decides who may
use it; a personal preference lives in your private scope and is never readable by another user.

## How Memory reaches an answer

Two tiers, and they behave differently:

- The **Core Block** is the always-on set rendered into every turn. It is small on purpose.
- **Recalled Memory** is fetched per turn by relevance to what you just asked. It is context for
  that answer only, never a standing instruction.

When an assistant needs something it cannot see in either tier — an older preference or a past
decision — it searches with `recall_memory`.

## Saving and removing

Assistants save with `update_memory` and remove with `delete_memory`. You can inspect and edit
your own Memory at `/settings/memory`, backed by `GET /api/v1/memory`, `PUT /api/v1/memory/{key}`,
and `DELETE /api/v1/memory/{key}`.

Good Memory is small, useful across turns, and safe to show the person who owns it — preferred
tone, recurring formatting choices, personal role context.

## Suggested memories are proposed, never assumed

After a chat finishes, TulipFarm mines it for durable facts. Nothing inferred is saved silently:
each one becomes a **Pending Memory** that waits for you. Suggested memories appear at
`/settings/memory` for you to confirm or deny, and denying one persists nothing.

Only things you state directly are stored without asking.

## Correcting how an Agent behaves

Memory normally holds statements of fact, never instructions. That ban is deliberate: text that
arrives from a document or a third party must never come back as an order the Agent follows.

Your own corrections are the one exception. When you tell an Agent "when I ask for the weekly
report, always include the churn number", it records that with `remember_correction` as a
**procedural** Assertion, and applies it on future turns. The engine only accepts a procedural
write that is explicit and stated by you — an Agent cannot infer a standing instruction from your
behaviour, or lift one out of a document it read. `POST /api/v1/memory/corrections` records the
same thing directly.

## Changing your mind

Memory keeps time. When a newer Assertion contradicts an older one, the old statement is not
overwritten — its validity is closed at the moment the new fact became true, so the record of what
was true, and when, survives. Asking what was true at a past moment returns the answer that held
then.

## Forgetting and erasing

Two different things, deliberately:

- **Forget** soft-removes an Assertion. The statement text is cleared but the row remains, so the
  lineage of what happened stays auditable.
- **Erase** hard-purges it, along with its evidence, any Pending Memory referencing it, its recall
  rows, and derived Episodes. Audit keeps counts only.

## Episodes

Beyond individual facts, TulipFarm keeps **Episodes** — durable summaries of what happened, what
was decided, and how it turned out, derived from a chat or a Run. Episodes are recallable from
other chats, which is what lets an assistant refer back to work you did somewhere else.

## What belongs in the system prompt

Static business facts belong in the system prompt, not Memory. The configured business name and
description from `soul.yaml` (`businessName`, `businessDescription`) identify the instance and are
rendered into the prompt as business context. They are not user preferences and should not be
stored as Memory just because onboarding collected them.

## What belongs in Records or Knowledge

Business state belongs in [Records](/docs/concepts/resources). Policies, procedures, and narrative
reference material belong in [Knowledge](/docs/concepts/knowledge) spaces and pages. Memory should
not become a hidden database of customers, tickets, policies, or operating rules.

## Agent scratch space is KV, not Memory

TulipFarm also exposes an Agent-scoped key-value store through `kv_get`, `kv_set`, `kv_delete`, and
`kv_list`, plus REST routes under `/api/v1/kv/{namespace}`. KV is durable scratch space for an
Agent or workflow namespace. It is not personal Memory and is not automatically rendered as user
preference context.
