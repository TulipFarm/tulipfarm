Your **soul** is where TulipFarm keeps everything you have taught it.

Every resource type you define, every agent you create, every routine, every skill, every connected
integration. They all live in your soul. It is the shape of your business, written down.

It is called a soul because it is what makes your instance *yours*. Two TulipFarm instances running
the same software with different souls behave like completely different products.

## You do not edit it

This is the important part.

You never open your soul, never edit a file, never learn its layout. You
[build by chatting](/docs/using-tulipfarm/how-building-by-chat-works), and the assistant writes
your soul for you.

Think of it the way you think of a document's version history. You know it is there. You are glad
it is there. You do not go and edit the history file by hand.

## It keeps every version

Underneath, a soul is a **git repository**, the same technology software teams use to track
changes to their work.

That choice buys you four things:

| What you get | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| **History** | Every change is recorded, with what changed and when. |
| **Comparison** | You can see exactly what a change did. |
| **Backup** | Point it at a remote and your configuration is safely copied off the machine. |
| **Recovery** | A change you regret can be undone. |

So when an agent creates a resource type for you, that is not a silent edit. It is a recorded
change you can go and read.

## What is in it, and what is not

Your soul holds **definitions**, how things are shaped and how they should behave.

Your database holds **data**, what actually happened and what your business knows.

| In your soul | In your database |
| --- | --- |
| Resource type schemas | The records themselves |
| Agents and their instructions | Chats and turns |
| Routines | runs and approvals |
| Skills | knowledge pages |
| Integration settings | memory, secrets, audit, identity |
| Guardrails and model settings | |

The distinction matters most when you think about backups. **A perfectly backed-up soul does not
protect your data.** If you lose the database, you lose your records, your knowledge, and your
history, even if every definition survives. See
[back up and restore](/docs/self-hosting/back-up-and-restore).

## Changes take effect immediately

There is no restart, no deploy, no publish button.

When an agent writes a change to your soul, the instance reloads and reconciles itself right away.
Ask for a new resource type and it exists (with screens generated for it) by the time the
assistant finishes replying.

## What stops a bad change

Soul writes are **direct**. Creating an agent changes your soul straight away, and the recorded
history is the review record.

The safety is in what happens next, not in a gate beforehand. Work an agent does can pause for
[approval](/docs/using-tulipfarm/approvals), every turn is a durable
[run](/docs/using-tulipfarm/runs) you can watch, and anything an agent could do is bounded by what
you are allowed to do.
